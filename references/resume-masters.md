# Create three or four real master résumés

Use the four structures in `../assets/resume-templates/` as starting points. These are content templates, not finished résumés. Replace every bracketed field with verified candidate evidence; remove sections that have no proof. Do not export or submit a template containing placeholders.

Choose paths from the candidate's target roles. The four structures represent different hiring questions:

| Structure | Hiring question | Lead evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Core practitioner | Can this person do the main job now? | Closest shipped work and scope |
| Adjacent transition | Can their proven method transfer to a new context? | Bridge project and honest boundary |
| Lead or manager | Can they raise the team's output and make decisions at wider scope? | Team adoption, people and system outcomes |
| Specialist or builder | Can they solve the hard domain or technical problem? | Inspectable artifact, implementation and constraints |

Do not create a lead master for someone without lead evidence or a specialist master from a tool list. Substitute a fourth credible target path if those do not fit. Three strong masters beat four nearly identical ones.

## Shared source and formatting rules

Maintain one evidence ledger as the source of truth. Keep official titles, employers, dates, education, and results consistent across all masters. Each master gets a distinct headline, summary, first proof, skills order, selected work, and bullet order. Change claims only when evidence supports the change. Keep a changelog of any new or corrected fact.

Use a simple single-column layout with standard headings, selectable text, ordinary fonts, and links that survive export. Avoid text boxes, icons as labels, decorative skill bars, tables for body content, and unexplained acronyms. Use reverse chronology unless the target path calls for a justified alternative. No universal page count: fit the candidate's experience, but make page one answer the target hiring question. Do not let a skills block bury the strongest proof.

For each master, write a 1–2 sentence positioning statement, then lead with 2–4 strongest approved bullets and an accessible work sample. A strong bullet says what the candidate changed, at what scope, and why it mattered; include a number only with baseline, period, and attribution the candidate can defend. Give collaborators credit. Separate measurable outcomes from unmeasured observations.

## Acceptance check

Read the first page as a recruiter in 15 seconds; identify role fit, level, and one proof. Read the featured case as a hiring manager; identify the candidate's decision, collaborators, shipped state, and result. Extract PDF and DOCX text; verify name, contact, titles, dates, bullet order, links, and no missing glyphs. Inspect the rendered pages for clipping and readability. Have the candidate approve all four master narratives before tailoring them to individual employers.
