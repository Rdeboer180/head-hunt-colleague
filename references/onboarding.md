# First-run screening and readiness

The goal is a trustworthy candidate profile and usable résumé masters. Begin with a choice: **upload personal writing** or **answer questions**. Offer a mix. Personal writing can include emails, project notes, portfolio narratives, presentation scripts, prior cover letters, or posts the candidate actually wrote. Ask them to redact client secrets and private third-party data. Prior résumés are useful for chronology but weak evidence of voice. Never treat AI-generated samples as an authentic voice without the candidate's edits and approval.

Suggested first message: “I’ll build a private record of your work and writing voice before tailoring applications. You can share 2–4 pieces you wrote yourself, answer a short interview about your projects and preferences, or combine both. Please also send your current résumé and 2–4 role examples you would actually consider. Redact anything confidential.”

Use available files and connected sources before asking the candidate to repeat facts. Ask a compact first batch, then follow up only on gaps affecting a target path. Let the candidate skip sensitive fields until a real form needs them.

## Screening questions

1. **Target and constraints:** What 2–4 role families and levels do you want? Which industries or problems attract you? Where can you work, and what remote, travel, relocation, schedule, compensation, authorization, or timing constraints matter? What roles are out of bounds?
2. **Career record:** For each role, what were your official title, employer, location, dates, team size, stakeholders, and actual responsibilities? What changed between promotions? Which employment gaps or transitions need a simple, truthful explanation?
3. **Proof:** Name 4–8 projects. For each: user/problem, your decision and contribution, collaborators, constraints, what shipped, result and measurement period, evidence source, link/access, confidentiality boundary, and what you would do differently. Ask for before/after context when a number is offered. Distinguish personal contribution from team result.
4. **Skills and level:** Which tools, methods, domains, management duties, and technical skills have you used in real work? Which would you avoid claiming? What scope can you independently own now?
5. **Voice:** If they supplied writing, identify repeated habits they want to keep or cut and confirm the read with them. Otherwise ask: How would you explain your best project to a friend? What would a colleague say you are unusually good at? Which work are you proud of and why? Which professional phrases would you never say? What tone should a recruiter hear? Ask for a short 150–250 word project account in their own words when answers remain generic.
6. **Review surface:** Which portfolio, code, work samples, LinkedIn, references, or public talks may employers see? Are links live, accessible, current, and permitted to share? What needs a password or a private version?
7. **Application history:** What has been submitted in the last 2–3 months, when, through which route, and with what outcome? What versions of the résumé were used? Ask for a tracker or email receipts when available.
8. **Form answers:** Record contact details, education, work authorization, sponsorship, salary rule, start date, relocation/travel, and recurring answers only when the candidate explicitly supplies or confirms them. Keep voluntary demographic answers optional and private. Never infer them.

## Build the private profile

Copy `../assets/candidate-profile-template.md` into the candidate's chosen workspace; keep the completed copy outside this skill. Include: target paths and constraints; career chronology; evidence ledger with sources and claim boundaries; voice guide with two short examples and phrases to avoid; approved links; recurring form answers; application history; open questions. Have the candidate review the summary for factual and voice errors. Do not require a personality test or make a mentor persona from thin evidence.

## Readiness gates

- **Ready to search:** target role families, location/work constraints, and at least a rough chronology exist. Mark unknowns in role comparisons.
- **Ready to build masters:** verified dates/titles, at least three substantial examples, skills with evidence, and one voice route are complete. A sample or interview route is enough; both are unnecessary.
- **Ready to apply:** the relevant master exists; the role's core requirements map to evidence or a named gap; claims and links are checked; candidate-specific voice guide is reviewed; contact and any required screening answers are confirmed; employer AI rules are understood. If a required fact is missing, ask for it and continue with independent research. Do not silently fill it from a generic template.

The candidate can authorize a narrow exception for an urgent application, but label the unverified item and avoid claiming it. Onboarding is complete only when the profile and at least three credible masters are reviewed, or the candidate deliberately narrows the number of paths.
